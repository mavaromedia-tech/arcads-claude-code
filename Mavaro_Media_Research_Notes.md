# Mavaro Media — Research Notes

Local business prospecting research · information checked **14 November 2026**

Companion to `Mavaro_Media_Prospecting_Database.pdf` and `Mavaro_Media_Prospecting_Database.xlsx`.

## 1. Summary

This database identifies 66 independently owned local businesses in and around Mississauga whose websites show a genuine, documented opportunity for redesign or digital-marketing help. It is the selective result of opening 245 business websites found through live web searches across more than 30 trades and service categories; 221 loaded successfully and were assessed, and about seven in ten were rejected because their sites were already good. Every lead page lists the specific faults observed, a tailored improvement plan, a cold-call opening line and the sources used.

- Leads: **66** — High 18, Medium 39, Low 9
- Websites opened: 245 · loaded and assessed: 221
- Research started in Mississauga and expanded outward to Brampton, Oakville, Burlington, Milton, Etobicoke and Caledon — all within roughly 35 km. Mississauga (41), Brampton (10), Burlington (5), Oakville (5), Etobicoke (3), Milton (1), Caledon (1).

## 2. Method

Each website was opened in a real Chromium browser twice on 14 November 2026: once at desktop size (1280 px) and once emulating a phone (390 px, touch). For every site the research recorded a screenshot of both views, load time, whether a mobile viewport is declared, whether the page overflows a phone screen, the share of text below 12 px, forms and tap-to-call links, broken images, the footer copyright year and the site platform. The About, Contact and Team pages of every shortlisted business were then read for addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, owner names and social links, and up to 14 internal links per site were tested for errors. Each screenshot was reviewed by eye for design quality, branding and calls to action before a business was accepted.

Candidate businesses were discovered with live web searches by trade and city (directories such as Yellow Pages and HomeStars block automated access, so they were not scraped). Because search engines favour well-optimised sites, about 70% of candidates turned out to have good websites and were rejected.

### Scoring

| Criterion | Points | What earns points |
|---|---|---|
| Website quality & need | 35 | Missing, offline or free-builder sites, obsolete design, mobile failures, broken elements, thin content. |

| Business suitability & value | 15 | Established, independently owned, good job values, and a category where web leads convert to revenue. |
| Contact accessibility | 10 | Publicly listed phone, email and a named owner or decision-maker. |

| Priority | Rule |
|---|---|
| High | Score 75–100. Strong opportunity. The website is missing, offline, on a free builder, or has significant, easily demonstrated problems. |
| Medium | Score 55–74. Potential opportunity. A working website with noticeable weaknesses — dated design, mobile faults, broken links or weak lead capture. |
| Low | Score below 55. Less obvious opportunity. A serviceable site with specific fixable issues; worth a call once stronger leads are worked. |

## 3. Limitations — read before calling

Verified: everything in each Website Audit and Technical Snapshot was observed directly on the live site on the date shown. Phone numbers, emails, addresses and owner names are copied from the business's own website unless the lead page names another source. Not verified: Google review counts and social-media posting activity — Google Maps and the social networks would not render in the research environment, so review counts are shown only where the business's own site displays them, and social links are listed as found without judging activity. No paid-advertising activity is claimed for any business. The "Google profile" link on each page opens a Google Maps search for the business so the caller can check reviews in one click. Owner names are given only where published; first-name-only and "listed contact" cases are labelled as such. Nothing has been guessed.

- **Google review counts and Google Business Profile status were not verified.** Google Maps would not render in the research browser. Each lead has a Google Maps *search* link, not a confirmed profile URL.
- **Social media activity was not assessed.** Links are listed where the business's own site links to them; "Not publicly available" means no link was found on the site, not that no profile exists.
- **LinkedIn** is listed only where the business site itself links to a profile.
- **Plain-http websites could not be opened** in the research environment (it only permits secure connections). Those businesses are on the unverified watchlist, not in the lead list.
- Load times were measured from a cloud data centre and will differ from a visitor in Mississauga; treat them as relative indicators.
- Lead scores are researcher judgement against the rubric, grounded in the measurements shown. They are not an automated metric.
- Owner names are included only where published by the business (or a named source). First-name-only cases are labelled.
